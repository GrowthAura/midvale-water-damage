Who do I call first for a sewer backup in Midvale?

Stop using water, then call your sewer provider to check the public main. Midvale has more than one provider, including Midvalley Sewer District, and your utility bill names yours.

The first call matters because the fix depends on where the blockage is. If the public main in the street is blocked, the provider clears it, and every flush in the house will keep adding to the backup until they do. Midvalley Sewer District, based at 160 East 7800 South, serves much of Midvale and north Sandy and asks residents to call the district first when sewage backs up. Other parts of the city are served by different providers, so check your bill or call Midvale City if you're not sure. Once the source is identified and stopped, the cleanup can start.

Stop, Then Call: The Right Order

  1. Stop all water use. No flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher. Every drain in the house feeds the backup.
  2. Keep people and pets away. Close the door to the affected area if you can.
  3. Call your sewer provider to check the public line.
  4. Turn off the furnace or central air if the backup is near ducts or the furnace, so contaminated air isn't spread through the house.
  5. Take photos of the drain, the water line and damaged items.
  6. Call (385) 337-3560 for cleanup. A crew is dispatched immediately.
  7. Call a plumber if the provider says the main is clear; the blockage is likely in your private line.

Why Sewage Is Dangerous

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, the most contaminated kind. It carries bacteria such as E. coli, viruses and parasites that can cause serious illness, especially for children, older adults and anyone with a weak immune system. It soaks into anything porous it touches, and the smell lingers long after the water is gone.

Household bleach doesn't solve this. It doesn't reach into drywall, carpet pad or wood, and mixing it with other cleaners can release toxic fumes. Proper sewage cleanup Midvale Utah homes need removes contaminated materials rather than trying to wash them.

Our Sewage Cleanup Midvale Utah Protocol

  1. Protect and contain. Crews wear protective gear, and the affected area is sealed off with plastic sheeting so contamination doesn't spread.
  2. Remove the waste. Liquid and solids are extracted and disposed of properly.
  3. Remove contaminated materials. Carpet, pad, drywall to above the waterline, insulation and other porous items come out.
  4. Clean and disinfect. Concrete, framing and hard surfaces are scrubbed and treated with EPA-registered disinfectants.
  5. Clean the air. HEPA air scrubbers run during and after the work to capture airborne particles and odor.
  6. Dry the structure. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry framing and concrete until readings match unaffected areas.
  7. Check and document. Moisture readings and photos record that the area is clean and dry before rebuild.

Floor Drain Backup in Older Midvale Homes

The basement floor drain is the lowest opening in most Midvale homes, so it is where a floor drain backup shows up first. In 1950s to 1970s ramblers and split-levels around Hillcrest, Midvale City Park and Union Park, that drain often sits in a finished laundry or utility room, right next to carpeted bedrooms. Sewage spreads under walls and into carpet before anyone sees it. A backwater valve, installed by a plumber after cleanup, can keep sewage from coming back up through the drain during the next blockage.

Toilet Sewage Overflow vs Main Line Backup

Not every overflow is a main line problem. A toilet sewage overflow from a single clogged toilet usually stays in one bathroom. But if the toilet backs up when the washer drains, or water rises in the tub when you flush, the blockage is further down the line and more backups are coming. Both need proper cleanup, but a main line backup usually affects more rooms and calls for a plumber or the sewer provider before the cleanup is finished.

Getting Rid of the Smell

Sewage odor comes from bacteria and from contaminated materials still in the room. Removing those materials is the first step. After that, disinfection, HEPA air scrubbing and drying take care of most odor. For stubborn smells in concrete or framing, sealing and additional odor treatment finish the job. Insurance: The Sewer Backup Endorsement

Many standard Utah homeowners policies do not cover sewer backups unless you add a water or sewer backup endorsement. That add-on usually covers cleanup and damaged belongings inside the home, often up to a set limit. Repairing or replacing the broken lateral itself may need separate service line coverage. Renters need their own renters policy for belongings.

Check your policy now, before you need it. If you already had a backup, our photos, material lists and moisture logs give your adjuster a clear record of what happened and what was removed.

Sewage Backup FAQ

Can I clean up a small sewage backup myself?

A few cups on a hard floor can be cleaned with gloves, eye protection and a disinfectant. Anything that reaches carpet, drywall or a larger area needs category 3 water cleanup with containment, material removal and disinfection.

How long does sewage cleanup take?

Cleanup and removal usually take 1 to 2 days, and drying takes about 3 to 5 more. Rebuild follows after that.

Is it safe to stay in my home?

Keep everyone out of the affected area. In most homes you can stay in clean parts of the house if the HVAC is off and the area is sealed. Small children and anyone with health problems may be better off staying elsewhere until cleanup is done.

Will the smell go away?

Yes, once contaminated materials are removed and the area is disinfected, dried and air-scrubbed. A smell that lasts usually means something contaminated was left behind.

Does homeowners insurance pay for sewer backup cleanup?

Only if your policy includes a sewer or water backup endorsement, in most cases. Check your declarations page or call your agent.
