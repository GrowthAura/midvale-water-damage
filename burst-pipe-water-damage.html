Where do pipes freeze first in Midvale homes?

Along uninsulated rim joists, cantilevered floors in split-levels, crawl spaces, garage walls, and outdoor hose bibs left connected to hoses after the first hard freeze.

Salt Lake Valley winters swing between mild days and long cold spells, and cold air often settles on the valley floor for days at a time. Pipes rarely freeze in the middle of a heated room. They freeze where cold outside air reaches them: the band of framing where the floor meets the foundation, floors that hang out past the wall below, unheated garages and crawl spaces, and the short run of pipe behind an outside faucet. When water in that spot freezes, it expands and the pressure splits the pipe, often a few feet away from the ice. The leak starts when it thaws, which is why so many burst pipes show up on the first warm afternoon after a cold night.

Turn It Off: Finding Your Shut-Off Fast

Every minute the water runs adds to the damage. Know these before you need them:

  • Main shut-off valve: usually in the basement or utility room, on the wall where the water line enters the house, often near the water heater. Turn it clockwise or a quarter turn for a lever valve.
  • Fixture valves: small valves under sinks, behind toilets and at the washer stop water to just that fixture.
  • Water heater valve: the cold-water line into the top of the tank has its own shut-off.
  • Curb box: the meter box near the street belongs to the water provider. Use it only if the inside valve fails, and call the city if you can't open it.

Once the water is off, open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain pressure from the lines.

Signs a Pipe Has Already Split

  • Water stopped or slowed to a trickle during a cold snap, then started again with a hissing or spraying sound.
  • A ceiling bulges, sags or drips, especially below a bathroom or outside wall.
  • Baseboards or flooring along an outside wall feel wet or cold.
  • Paint bubbles or drywall softens near a hose bib or under a window.
  • The water meter keeps spinning with every faucet off.
  • A sudden jump in the water bill with no new use.

Pipe Burst in Wall: Why Surface Drying Isn't Enough

When a pipe bursts in a wall, water runs down the inside of the cavity, soaks the insulation and pools on the bottom plate and subfloor. The drywall face may look dry while the inside stays wet. Box fans and open windows can't reach it.

We use thermal imaging and moisture meters to trace the water, then dry the cavity directly: baseboards come off, small holes are drilled behind them, and dry air is pushed through the wall. Wet insulation is removed when readings say it can't be dried. Opening a wall a little, in the right place, saves much larger repairs later. Water Leak Detection (/water-leak-detection/)

Sudden Burst vs Slow Leak: What Insurance Usually Covers

Most Utah homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a pipe that froze and split overnight. They usually do not cover damage from a slow leak that went on for weeks, or the cost of the pipe itself. Many policies also expect reasonable steps to keep a home heated in winter.

That is why quick action and good records matter. Dated photos, moisture readings and a clear record of where the water started help show the damage was sudden. If the pipe served more than one unit, see Condo & Apartment Water Damage (/condo-apartment-water-damage/).

Hour by Hour After a Pipe Bursts

Knowing what water does over time explains why the first call matters so much.

  • First hour: water spreads across the floor, soaks carpet pad and starts climbing drywall. Ceilings below the leak may begin to sag.
  • Hours 1 to 24: drywall swells and softens, paint bubbles, wood trim and cabinet bases start to warp, and water reaches rooms you can't see from the leak.
  • Days 1 to 3: clean water starts turning into gray water, musty odors appear, and mold can begin growing on wet drywall and insulation.
  • Week 1 and beyond: subfloors and framing can stay wet, mold spreads inside walls, and repairs grow from a few feet of drywall to whole rooms.

When extraction and drying start in the first few hours, most burst pipe jobs stay small. When they start days later, the same leak can mean removing ceilings, floors and cabinets.

Freeze-Proofing Before the Next Cold Snap

  • Disconnect garden hoses and drain outside faucets before the first hard freeze.
  • Insulate pipes in crawl spaces, garages and along rim joists.
  • Seal gaps where cold air reaches pipes, especially around sill plates and cantilevered floors.
  • Keep the thermostat at a steady temperature at night and when you travel.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls during cold spells.
  • Let a faucet on an outside wall drip during the coldest nights.
  • Know where your main shut-off is and make sure it turns.

Burst Pipe FAQ

How do I know if a frozen pipe has burst?

If water stopped during a freeze and comes back with hissing, or you see wet spots, bulging ceilings or a moving meter with everything off, a pipe has likely split. Shut off the main and call.

Should I use a hair dryer or torch to thaw a pipe?

Never use an open flame. A hair dryer or space heater at a safe distance is fine, but if the pipe may already be split, shut off the main first so thawing doesn't start a flood.

How long does drying take after a burst pipe?

Most rooms dry in about 3 to 5 days. Inside walls, ceilings and subfloors can take a little longer.

Do I need a plumber and a restoration crew?

Yes. A plumber repairs the pipe, and a restoration crew removes the water and dries the structure. We work around the plumber's schedule so walls aren't closed until both are done.

What if the burst pipe was in the unit above me?

Call the property manager or HOA, then call us. We dry your unit and keep records separate from the source unit.
