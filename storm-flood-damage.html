Why do Midvale streets and basements flood in summer storms?

Short, heavy summer cloudbursts overload older storm drains, sending runoff down driveways into window wells, garages and basements, especially in low spots toward the Jordan River.

Midvale's storm drain system is one of the city's oldest pieces of infrastructure, and the city has been working on upgrades because aging lines can back up even in moderate rain. During a cloudburst, gutters fill, inlets clog with leaves and debris, and water rises over curbs. It follows the slope of the land, which in Midvale runs generally west toward the Jordan River. Homes with driveways sloping toward the garage, basement stairwells, window wells or low backyards collect that water first. In spring, the same low spots take on snowmelt runoff from the Wasatch, and groundwater rises under saturated clay soil.

Flood Damage Restoration Midvale Utah: Our Process

Every flood damage restoration Midvale Utah job follows the same safe order.

  1. Make it safe. Power is shut off to flooded areas, and gas appliances that sat in water are flagged for a check before use.
  2. Pump out the water. Submersible pumps remove deep water, then extractors pull water from floors.
  3. Remove mud and debris. Silt is shoveled and squeegeed out before drying starts, because drying mud just bakes it in.
  4. Remove contaminated materials. Carpet, pad, drywall and insulation touched by floodwater come out.
  5. Clean and disinfect floors, framing and every hard surface.
  6. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers until readings match dry areas.
  7. Document everything with photos, moisture readings and a removed-materials list.
  8. Rebuild. Water Damage Repair

Storm Flooding Cleanup Safety: What Not to Do

Storm flooding cleanup carries risks that clean-water jobs don't. Until the crew arrives:

  • Don't walk through floodwater near outlets, appliances or a breaker panel.
  • Don't let children or pets play in or near floodwater.
  • Don't run the furnace if it sat in water; have it checked first.
  • Don't use gas-powered pumps or generators indoors or in a garage.
  • Wear rubber boots and gloves if you must enter the area.
  • Wash hands and anything that touched the water.

Flood Insurance vs Homeowners Insurance in Utah

This is the part that surprises most Midvale homeowners. Standard homeowners policies usually do not cover flooding from surface water, overflowing canals or rising groundwater. That kind of damage is typically covered only by a separate flood insurance policy, through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or a private insurer. Flood policies have a waiting period before coverage starts, so buying one after a storm is forecast is usually too late.

Some storm damage is still covered by homeowners policies, such as wind or hail damaging a roof and letting rain inside. Sewer backups caused by a storm usually need a sewer backup endorsement. Sewage Backup Cleanup

Whatever coverage you have, our photos, moisture readings and lists of removed materials give your adjuster what they need.

Protecting Your Midvale Property Before the Next Storm

  • Clear gutters, downspouts and the storm drain inlet in front of your home before summer.
  • Extend downspouts away from the foundation and window wells.
  • Add covers to window wells and keep their gravel drains clear.
  • Keep basement stairwell drains free of leaves and debris.
  • Raise belongings in basements and garages off the floor.
  • Keep sandbags on hand for garage doors and walkouts if your lot sits low.
  • Ask about a backwater valve if your home has had a sewer backup during storms.

What to Photograph for a Flood or Storm Claim

Good photos make a big difference to any claim. Before cleanup starts, capture:

  • The water line on walls, furniture and appliances.
  • Where water entered: window wells, doors, garage, stairwell or drain.
  • The outside conditions: flooded street, clogged drain inlet, overflowing gutters.
  • Damaged belongings, with brand and model labels where you can.
  • Mud and debris left behind.
  • The date and time, or a news alert or weather notice showing the storm.

Our crew adds moisture readings, daily drying logs and a list of everything removed, so your adjuster has the full picture.

Storm & Flash Flood FAQ

Is storm water really contaminated?

Yes. It carries soil, chemicals, oil and bacteria from yards and streets, so it is handled as Category 3 water.

Can carpet be saved after a flash flood?

Usually not. Carpet and pad soaked by floodwater are removed for safety.

Does homeowners insurance cover flash floods in Utah?

Usually not. Surface flooding is typically covered only by flood insurance.

How long does flood cleanup take?

Removal and cleaning usually take 1 to 3 days, and drying about 3 to 5 more.

Should I start cleaning up mud myself?

If it's safe, removing loose mud early helps. Wear boots and gloves, and leave walls and flooring decisions to the crew.
