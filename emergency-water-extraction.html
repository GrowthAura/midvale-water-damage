How fast should standing water be removed from a Midvale home?

Within the first few hours. After 24 to 48 hours, drywall, carpet pad and subfloors absorb the water and mold risk rises, even in Utah's dry air.

Water does not sit still. It runs to the lowest point, slides under baseboards, and soaks into the pad beneath carpet before it shows on top. Drywall acts like a sponge and can wick water well above the visible waterline within a day. Particleboard subfloors and cabinet bases swell and lose strength. Clean water from a supply line also changes over time: after about 48 hours, bacteria growth turns it into gray water, which means more materials have to come out instead of being dried in place. That is why the clock matters more than anything else on an extraction job.

What Happens in the First 60 Minutes on Site

Every Midvale extraction follows the same order, so nothing gets missed while the water is still moving.

  1. Minutes 0 to 10: make it safe. We confirm the water source is off, isolate power to wet rooms, check gas appliances near the water and mark slip hazards.
  2. Minutes 10 to 20: map the water. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water traveled under floors, behind walls and into the rooms next door.
  3. Minutes 20 to 45: extract. Standing water comes out first, then the water trapped in carpet and pad, starting with the wettest areas.
  4. Minutes 45 to 60: protect and set drying. Furniture is lifted onto blocks, belongings are moved, and air movers and dehumidifiers are placed for water extraction and drying to run around the clock.

Every step is photographed and logged, so your adjuster sees exactly what was found and done.

Clean, Gray or Sewage: How the Water Category Changes Extraction

The source decides how the water is handled.

  • Clean water (Category 1): a burst supply line, a failed water heater or a fridge line. Most materials can be dried in place if work starts quickly.
  • Gray water (Category 2): washers, dishwashers and toilet overflows without waste. Pad comes out, and surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products.
  • Black water (Category 3): sewer backups, storm runoff and river or canal water. Porous materials are removed, and the area is disinfected and air-scrubbed. See Sewage Backup Cleanup.

If you are not sure what kind of water you have, treat it as contaminated until it is tested and keep children and pets out.

While You Wait: Do This, Not That

Do:

  • Shut off the main water valve or the fixture valve.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  • Move electronics, rugs, photos and papers to a dry room.
  • Take photos and video of every wet area before anything is moved.

Don't:

  • Use a household vacuum. A shop vac can help with a small spill, but it isn't built for flooded house water removal and leaves the pad soaked.
  • Walk through water near outlets, power strips or appliances that are still plugged in.
  • Run the furnace or central air if the water came from a sewer line.
  • Pull up carpet yourself; it can tear the backing and make it harder to save.

Emergency Water Extraction FAQ

How long does water extraction take?

Most residential extraction takes about 2 to 6 hours, depending on the volume of water and the size of the area. Drying then runs for about 3 to 5 days.

Do I need to leave my home during extraction?

Usually not for clean water. For sewage or heavy contamination, we recommend keeping children, pets and anyone with breathing problems out of the affected area.

Can you remove water from under hardwood or vinyl plank floors?

Yes. Floor drying mats pull water out from under hardwood, and vinyl or laminate planks can be lifted so the concrete below dries properly.

What if the water is coming from the unit above me?

Call the property manager or HOA, then call us. We extract and dry your unit and document it separately from the source unit.

Will mold grow after extraction?

Not if drying is done properly. We monitor moisture every day and only stop when readings match the dry parts of your home.

Do you work nights, weekends and holidays?

Yes. Calls are answered 24/7, and a crew is dispatched immediately.
