Signs Your Crawl Space Is Wet

  • A musty or earthy smell inside the house, stronger in rooms over the crawl space.
  • Floors that feel soft, bouncy or cold.
  • Hardwood cupping or vinyl lifting on the floor above.
  • Higher humidity, condensation on windows or more bugs indoors.
  • Water pressure dropping or a water bill rising with no new use.
  • Standing water or mud visible through the crawl space vent or access hatch.

If you notice any of these, look through the access hatch with a flashlight, but don't go in if there is standing water near wiring or a sewage smell.

Why a Wet Crawl Space Is a Problem for the Whole House

Air from a crawl space rises into the living space above it. When the crawl space is wet, that air carries moisture, musty odors and mold spores into the house. Wet floor joists lose strength over time, and constantly damp wood invites rot and wood-boring insects. Subfloors swell and floors above start to squeak, sag or cup. Wet insulation between the joists falls down, and pests move in. Drying a crawl space quickly protects the structure and the air your family breathes.

Crawl Space Vapor Barrier After Drying

A crawl space vapor barrier is a heavy plastic sheet laid over the dirt floor and often run partway up the walls. It slows ground moisture from rising into the space and gives a cleaner surface for anyone working there later. After a water event, the old barrier is usually removed because water gets trapped under it. Once the space is dry, a new barrier helps keep it that way. Some homes also benefit from a small dehumidifier or a sump pit if groundwater is a repeat problem.

Crawl Space Hazards to Know Before Going In

A wet crawl space can be dangerous, so it helps to know what's down there before anyone crawls in:

  • Electrical: old wiring, junction boxes and extension cords can sit in or near standing water.
  • Sewage: a broken drain line means bacteria in the water and soil, and on any surface it touched.
  • Mold: spores from long-wet wood and insulation can irritate lungs and eyes.
  • Tight spaces: low clearance, sharp nails, old ductwork and debris make it easy to get stuck or hurt.
  • Pests: wet crawl spaces attract rodents, spiders and insects.

If you smell sewage or see standing water near wiring, stay out and wait for the crew. We enter with protective gear and lighting and check each of these hazards first.

Preventing the Next Crawl Space Flood

  • Insulate pipes in the crawl space and close foundation vents during cold snaps.
  • Keep downspouts and sprinklers pointed away from the foundation and vents.
  • Slope soil away from the house so water drains out, not in.
  • Have old galvanized lines and clay drain lines inspected and replaced as needed.
  • Install a vapor barrier once the space is dry.
  • Check the crawl space with a flashlight each spring and fall, especially after heavy snowmelt.
  • Add a battery water-leak alarm near the water heater or main line if they run through the crawl space.

Crawl Space FAQ

Can I dry my crawl space with a fan?

A fan moves air but doesn't remove moisture. Without dehumidifiers, wet joists and soil can stay damp for weeks.

Is water in a crawl space covered by insurance?

Water from a sudden pipe burst is often covered. Groundwater and slow leaks often are not. Our photos and readings help your adjuster decide.

How long does crawl space drying take?

Usually about 3 to 7 days, depending on the size, how wet the wood is and the weather.

Will a vapor barrier stop all water?

It slows moisture rising from the ground but does not stop flooding from pipes, sewer lines or surface water.

Do wet floor joists need to be replaced?

Usually not. Most wet floor joists can be dried and treated. Replacement is only needed if rot has already weakened them.
