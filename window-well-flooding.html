Why do Midvale window wells fill with water and flood the basement?

Window wells catch snowmelt, roof runoff and summer cloudbursts. When the gravel drain clogs or the yard slopes toward the house, water rises above the sill and pours inside.

A window well is a metal or plastic liner around a basement window, set below ground with gravel at the bottom. That gravel is supposed to let water soak away. Over time it fills with silt, leaves and grass clippings, and in many Midvale homes it was never connected to a real drain in the first place. Add a downspout nearby, a lawn sprinkler aimed the wrong way, or snow drifting into an uncovered well, and the well becomes a bucket. Once the water reaches the bottom of the window frame, it finds the gaps around the frame and runs down the inside wall.

What to Do the Minute a Window Well Overflows

  • Move people and pets away from the window and out of standing water.
  • Turn off power to the room if outlets or cords are near the water and the panel is safe to reach.
  • If it is safe outside, scoop water out of the well with a bucket to lower it below the sill.
  • Point downspouts and sprinklers away from the well.
  • Move furniture, rugs, electronics and boxes off the wet floor.
  • Take photos inside and outside before anything is moved.

Do not break the window glass to drain the well into the room. It turns a slow leak into a flood, and the water outside is not clean.

Window Well Leak Into Basement Midvale: The Usual Causes

Most window well floods have more than one cause. Finding them all is how a window well leak into basement Midvale homeowners face once doesn't happen again.

Window Well Drain Clogged

The gravel at the bottom of the well packs down and fills with silt over the years. When the window well drain is clogged, water that should soak away just keeps rising.

Downspouts and Roof Runoff

A downspout that empties a few feet from a window well sends the water from a whole roof section straight into it. One summer cloudburst can fill a well in minutes.

Yard Grading and Settled Soil

Soil around new foundations settles for years. When the ground near the house sinks lower than the yard, rain and snowmelt run toward the wells instead of away.

Snow Drifts and Uncovered Wells

Winter wind packs snow into open wells. When it melts in a warm spell, the water has nowhere to go.

Failing Window Seals and Frames

Even a well with a little water can leak if the caulk around the frame is cracked or the frame itself has rotted. An egress window leak often shows as a stain under the window or swollen trim long before a full flood.

Rising Groundwater

During wet springs, groundwater can rise into the bottom of the well from below, especially on lower lots on Midvale's west side.

Basement Bedrooms and Egress Windows

Most Midvale window wells sit outside basement bedrooms, because a bedroom needs an egress window large enough to climb out of. That means the room that floods is usually a bedroom, with carpet, a bed, clothes and closets right under the window. The larger the window, the more water can come through when the well fills.

Water runs down the wall below the window and soaks the carpet and pad along that wall first. It also soaks the bottom of the drywall, the window framing and the insulation beneath the sill. Those spots dry slowly and are where mold starts if they are missed.

How We Clean Up Window Well Flooding

  1. Lower the water outside. We pump or remove water from the well so no more comes in.
  2. Extract inside. Standing water and the water in carpet and pad come out with truck-mounted or portable extractors.
  3. Check the category. Runoff can carry soil, fertilizer and lawn chemicals, so dirty water is handled as contaminated, not as clean water.
  4. Map the walls. Moisture meters and thermal imaging find water inside the wall under the window and in the rooms next to it.
  5. Open only what's needed. Wet insulation under the sill and soaked drywall at the base of the wall are removed when readings say so.
  6. Dry the framing. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the window buck, studs and floor until readings match dry areas.

Water Coming Through Basement Window Midvale: When It's More Than Rain

Sometimes water coming through basement window Midvale homeowners notice has nothing to do with a storm. A broken sprinkler line, a hose left running, a neighbor's yard draining onto your side, or groundwater rising in spring can all fill a well on a dry day. If the well fills without rain, look at sprinklers, irrigation and the slope of the yard next door before anything else.

Midvale's Two Window Well Seasons

Spring (about March to June): Wasatch snowmelt soaks the valley's clay soil, groundwater rises and late-season storms hit ground that is already full. Wells fill slowly from below and from the sides.

Summer (about July to September): Short, heavy cloudbursts dump a lot of water at once. Roofs, driveways and sloped yards send it straight into wells, and older storm drains can back up and overflow onto lawns.

Winter adds a third, smaller risk: snow drifting into uncovered wells and melting all at once during a warm spell.

Window Well Problems by Midvale Home Type

Window wells fail differently depending on when and how a home was built.

1950s to 1970s Ramblers

Many older ramblers near Historic Main Street and East Midvale have small, shallow window wells added long after the house was built, often with no drain at all. Corrugated metal liners rust through at the bottom, and soil leaks in with the water.

Split-Levels in Hillcrest and Union Park

Split-levels put lower-level windows closer to grade, so even a short downpour can reach the sill. Wells along the side yard often sit right under a downspout or beside a driveway that drains toward the house.

Newer Homes and Townhomes

Newer homes and townhomes around Bingham Junction and Jordan Bluffs use larger egress wells with plastic liners. Their gravel is usually better, but backfill around a newer foundation keeps settling for years, and the ground beside the well can sink below the rest of the yard.

Basement Rentals

When a basement is rented, the tenant may not see the well fill until water is already inside. Owners can prevent the worst of it with covers and a quick check of every well each spring and fall, when leaves and snow pile up.

Window Well Flooding FAQ

Does homeowners insurance cover window well flooding in Utah?

It depends on the policy and the cause. Many policies exclude surface water and groundwater, while some cover sudden events. Our photos and moisture logs help your adjuster decide either way.

Can I just dry the carpet with fans?

Fans dry the surface, but water under the pad and inside the wall under the window stays wet. That trapped moisture is where mold and odors start.

Should I break the window to let water out of the well?

No. It sends all the water into the room at once and the water carries dirt from outside. Lower the water from outside instead.

How long does drying take after a window well flood?

Most rooms dry in about 3 to 5 days. Wet framing under the window can take a little longer.

Will a window well cover stop all flooding?

It stops most rain and snow from entering the well, but not groundwater or runoff from a badly sloped yard. Covers work best together with good drainage and downspout extensions.
