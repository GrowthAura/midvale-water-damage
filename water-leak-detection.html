How do you find a hidden water leak in a Midvale home without opening walls?

Thermal imaging shows cool, wet areas behind drywall, and moisture meters confirm them, so the leak path is traced with few or no holes before repairs start.

Wet materials show up cooler than dry ones on a thermal camera because evaporation pulls heat from them. That creates a pattern on the screen: a cool band down a wall, a spreading patch on a ceiling, a line along the floor. Thermal imaging doesn't see water directly, so every cool spot is checked with moisture meters. Pinless meters scan surfaces quickly; pin meters confirm how wet a material is at a specific point. Together they map exactly where water has traveled, which tells us, the plumber and your insurer where the source is and how far the damage goes.

Signs You Have a Hidden Leak

  • Your water bill jumps with no change in use.
  • The water meter moves when every faucet and appliance is off.
  • You hear running or dripping water when nothing is on.
  • A musty smell won't go away.
  • Paint bubbles, wallpaper peels or drywall softens.
  • Floors feel soft, cupped or warped.
  • A ceiling stain comes back after it was painted.
  • Mold spots appear near baseboards, under sinks or in corners.

The Simple Meter Test

Before calling, you can check for a leak yourself:

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance, irrigation system and the water softener regeneration.
  2. Find the water meter, usually in a box near the street.
  3. Note the reading or watch the small leak indicator dial.
  4. Wait about 30 minutes without using any water.
  5. Check again. If the reading changed or the indicator moved, water is leaking somewhere.

This tells you a leak exists. Finding where it is takes the tools below.

How to Know Walls Are Dry

Touch isn't enough. A wall can feel dry on the surface while the inside of the cavity stays wet. How to know walls are dry:

  1. Take readings in a dry area of the same home to set a baseline.
  2. Take readings in the affected area at the bottom, middle and top of the wall.
  3. Check the framing behind the drywall with a pin meter where possible.
  4. Compare: the wall is dry when readings match the baseline.
  5. Recheck after a day or two to make sure readings hold.

This is the same method used to decide when drying is finished after any water event, and it's what keeps mold from starting later.

Leak Detection or a Plumber: Who to Call First?

Call a plumber first when you know where the leak is and just need the pipe fixed, such as a dripping supply line under a sink.

Call for leak detection and moisture mapping first when:

  • You know there's a leak but not where it is.
  • Water has already spread into walls, ceilings or floors.
  • You need to know how far the damage goes before deciding what to open.
  • An insurance claim or a dispute with a neighbor, landlord or HOA depends on where the water came from.
  • A plumber fixed a leak but you still smell mustiness or see stains.

In many cases, the best order is detection first, plumber second, then drying. That keeps holes small and repairs targeted.

Water Leak Detection FAQ

Can thermal imaging see through walls?

Not exactly. It shows surface temperature differences that point to wet areas behind the surface, which are then confirmed with moisture meters.

Will you need to cut holes?

Often not. When an opening is needed, the map keeps it as small and as targeted as possible.

Do you fix the leaking pipe?

How long does leak detection take?

Can moisture mapping help my insurance claim?

Yes. It shows where water went and how far it spread, which helps your adjuster set the scope.
