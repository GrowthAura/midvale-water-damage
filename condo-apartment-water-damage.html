Who pays when water leaks from an upstairs condo in Midvale?

It depends on where the leak started and your HOA documents. Usually the HOA master policy covers shared structure and the owner's HO-6 covers the unit interior and belongings.

Utah condo and townhome communities run on two layers of rules: state law, including Utah's Condominium Ownership Act, and the HOA's own governing documents, usually called the declaration or CC&Rs. Those documents say which parts of the building belong to the association and which belong to each owner, and they set how the master policy deductible is handled. That split decides who pays for what. A pipe inside a shared wall, a roof leak or a common drain stack often falls to the association. A washer hose, dishwasher line or toilet inside one unit often falls to that owner. Renters are covered only for their own belongings and only through a renters policy. Because every community's documents differ, the first step is always clear records of where the water started and where it went.

Water Leak From Upstairs Unit: What to Do First

If you find a water leak from upstairs unit ceilings or walls, take these steps in order:

  1. Knock on the unit above or call them. If no one answers, call the property manager or HOA emergency line.
  2. Shut off power to the wet room if water is near lights or outlets.
  3. Protect belongings. Move electronics, furniture and papers out of the drip zone.
  4. Take photos and video of the ceiling, walls and floor right away.
  5. Call (385) 337-3560 so a crew is dispatched while the source is found.
  6. Notify your own insurer and, if you rent, your landlord.
  7. Keep a written log of who you spoke to and when.

Don't wait for the other parties to sort out responsibility before drying starts. Every hour the water sits makes the claim bigger for everyone.

How Water Moves Through Midvale's Stacked Buildings

About a third of Midvale's housing units sit in buildings with 10 or more units, and many more are townhomes that share walls. Water in these buildings follows paths you can't see:

  • Down through floors: from a bathroom, laundry or kitchen into the ceiling of the unit below.
  • Along shared walls: through wall cavities, plumbing chases and gaps around pipes into the unit next door.
  • Through drain stacks: a blockage in a shared stack backs sewage into the lowest unit on that line. Sewage Backup Cleanup
  • Along concrete slabs: under flooring in podium-style buildings, water can travel across a floor before it shows.
  • From the roof or a sprinkler line: into top-floor units, then down to lower floors.

That's why we check the units above, below and beside the source, not just the one that called.

Apartment Water Damage Midvale: Owners, Renters and Landlords

Apartment water damage Midvale renters face sits in an awkward spot. The landlord's policy covers the building, but not the tenant's belongings. The tenant's renters policy covers their belongings, but not the walls or floors. When we're called to an apartment, we:

  • Photograph and list the tenant's damaged belongings separately.
  • Document the structure, floors, walls and cabinets for the owner.
  • Note the source and the date the leak was found.
  • Share the records with the right party for each part of the claim.

More than half of Midvale homes are rented, so this split comes up on almost every multi-unit job. Clear records keep the tenant, landlord and property manager on the same page.

HO-6 Water Damage: What Unit Owners Should Know

An HO-6 policy covers the parts of a condo the owner is responsible for, usually interior walls, flooring, cabinets and fixtures, plus personal belongings and liability. HO-6 water damage claims often involve the master policy deductible too, because many associations can pass part of that deductible to the owner whose unit caused the loss. Check two things now, before a leak: how much "walls-in" coverage your HO-6 has, and whether it includes loss assessment coverage for HOA deductibles.

Fire Sprinkler Discharges

Newer apartment and condo buildings have fire sprinkler systems. A frozen sprinkler line, a damaged head or an accidental hit can release a large volume of water fast, often into hallways and several units at once. Sprinkler water sits in the pipes for a long time and is not clean, so it is handled as gray water. Shutting off the system is a job for building management or the fire protection company. Once it's off, we extract and dry every affected area.

Preventing Unit-to-Unit Leaks

  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided stainless hoses.
  • Put drain pans under upstairs washers and water heaters.
  • Add water-leak alarms in laundry rooms, under sinks and near water heaters.
  • Shut off the washer valves when you travel.
  • Report small stains or drips to the HOA or manager right away.
  • Keep your unit heated in winter, even when empty.

Condo & Apartment Water Damage FAQ

My upstairs neighbor caused the leak. Do I still call my own insurer?

Usually yes. Your insurer can handle your claim and pursue the other party's insurer if needed.

Can drying start before the HOA decides who pays?

Yes, and it should. Delay makes the damage and the claim bigger.

I rent. Is my landlord responsible for my belongings?

Usually not. Your belongings are covered by your renters policy, if you have one.

Will you work with our property manager?

Yes. We coordinate access, share records and give the manager one summary for the building.

How long does drying take in a condo?

Most units dry in about 3 to 5 days. Concrete floors in podium buildings can take longer.
